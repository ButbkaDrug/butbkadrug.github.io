### OBSERVATION
What we see is that Paul expresses his  gratefulness for the financial support he received from the people in church. But he emphasizes that financial well being doesn't hold central place neither in his life, his ministry or the relationships. Simply put he says "I'm happy you care for me. But it's not because of money. Money, or financial wellness do not matter to me".

In verse 11 Paul doesn't say: "I don't have needs". He says that "Needs do not rule over my life". "They do not dictate my direction."

This scripture is a hard peal to swallow for the prosperity gospel fans out there. But it is what it is.

Paul says that life is full of ups and downs. And it's not that I never experience need. But I've learned **to be content** with whatever is going on.

Where "**to be content**" meanings - being satisfied with what one have at the moment. Meaning inner happiness regardless of the outer obstacles.

So, apparently it's not about twisting God's hands to give me what I want. Or what I think I need. But rather being **contents** with what I have.

#### Philippians 4:12
> ¹²I know how to get along with little, and I also know how to live in prosperity; in any and every *circumstance* **I have learned the secret**... ~~of being filled and going hungry, both of having abundance and suffering need.~~

Paul says: **I know** and **I have learned**: Which implies that he have enough practice to posses this skills. Because being a Jew, when he says *I know* it means something. Which not always resembles with our idea of "*knowing*". Because we have adopted the Greek style of leering. Which mostly comes down to observation. Leering through careful observation of the world.

### PLAYING GUITAR
For example you see me playing guitar. You saw this process enough time to say "I know how to play guitar". You press the strings with your left hand. You pluck strings with your right hand and this is how one plays guitar. But, it doesn't mean that if I will give you a guitar you can play it. 

### GREEK LEARING
In Greek culture to say: **I know how to do it** and to be able to **actually do it** are not the same thing. You can possess the knowledge how something is done or of a particular processes, but it down’t mean that you possess the skill of doing it. It down’t mean that you have done it yourself before. Again because **to know** is **to see** in Ancient Greek Culture. So much so that the words you used to say I know is literally *I see*.

In English, we kinda inherited that. We still say **I see what you mean**, right?

### JEWISH
But Jewish style of learning is different. When you say, **I know** it implies that you possess *the skill* of doing something and not the knowledge alone. **You learn by doing**, by practicing. So in the Jewish worldview, when one say, I know how to play guitar. It means he can actually play it.

### BACK TO PAUL
When Paul says, I know how to get along with a little. What he means is that he practiced it a lot. It means that he knows how to live when you do not have much, because  he actually experienced that enough times to figure it out. Same with abundance. Not only he knows that in theory you can have more money that you actually need. But he actually practiced it.

So when Paul says that he can do everything. What he means is that he can **endure all things**. That no matter whether he have abundance or suffering need, whether his full or hungry, his strength, his passion his energy is still the same.

Because the source of his power, desire, passion, identity and meaning is not in finances. It is in the Lord, therefore no matter what he’s financial condition is. It never stops him from doing the Lord’s work.
