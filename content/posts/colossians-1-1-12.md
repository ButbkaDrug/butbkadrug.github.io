### START
Great! Let's go through the verses one by one'ish. And let's practice one of the most valuable skills, that someone who studding the bible can have. Making observations. Not a conclusions, not interpretations(those will come later). But let's observe what this text is actually saying.

### INTRODUCTION
This letter starts with an introduction as it should. In the verse 1 apostle Paul introduces himself and brother Timothy.  

And if we will have a closer look at how he introduces himself. What we will see?

### Colossians 1:1 (NASB95)   
> Paul, **an apostle** of Jesus Christ **by the will of God**...

Paul introduces himself as **an apostle** of Jesus Christ. Apostle is a messenger, spokesman, the one who were sent forth with orders and **authority** to fulfill those orders on the behalf of the **master**.

Paul introduces himself as a person with authority. And the proof of his authority contained in the second part of the verse. He says I am an apostle by **the will of God**.

In other words, he says "*my authority comes from the Lord. I was appointed by God himself*". And it’s nothing but  truth, considering the story of apostle Paul. If you never explored it, I highly recommend it’s all in the book of act.

### COMPARTMENT 
But today I want us to notice the difference. Because, we just finished the letter to Philippines. And if we would compare how Paul introduces himself there, to the introduction in our current letter, that's what we will see:  

### Philippians 1:1 (NASB95)
Paul and Timothy, **bond-servants**(or slaves) of Christ Jesus…

Interesting! He doesn't speak about himself as and the apostle selected by God. He just says I’m a slave of Christ, just as you guys. That’s it.

And it’s not about Paul, changing his view of himself. Getting a bit more humbled over time or something. No, both letters were written in approximately the same time. One after another. So, not very much changed there.

But One may argue that:

> "It's just a greetings! Yes, they are different, but who cares??? Not a big deal..." Right?

### NOT JUST GREETINGS
No, it's not just a greeting. It's not just a "*white noise*", filler words that supposed to be there, because this is how you write letters.

Often we do treat parts of the bible like that. Mostly because in our modern world, we are so used to a lot of noise. You go on Social Media and especially YouTube. And dozens of people try to speak into your life. Most often trying to sell you something or get your time and attention. And they say a bunch of words with very little meaning.

So much so, that we even have a term for it. Yaping. They just yap to fill up the required time... To please the algorithms. And our brains adapted to it. Maybe without you even knowing. Brain cranks up noise filtering mechanisms. And we stop paying attention.

Unfortunately, when it comes to reading and studying the Bible, that "filter" is still engaged. It’s still filtering. And we lose valuable information.

We should learn to turn our "filters" off because bible doesn't really have **filler words**. Phrases without the  meaning and a purpose.  

### IT HAVE A PURPOSE
And this introduction have a purpose as well. There is a good reason why apostle Paul chooses this title over all other ways he could introduce himself. I challenge you to think about **why**? Why he speaks about himself in this manner? And hopefully by the end of our time today you will have the answer, before I give it away.

But now let’s continue into the verse 2:

#### Colossians 1:2 (NASB95)
> To the saints and faithful brethren in Christ who are at Colossae: Grace to you and peace from God our Father.

And again on one hand, it looks like a normal greeting, which is typical for this type of letters.

But let's keep our filters down and  pay closer attention to the way Paul describes his audience. This translation says:

“To the **saints** and **faithful** brethren in Christ..”

Which is in my opinion can be a bit confusing. You might think that the **Saints** and **faithful breathing** are two different groups of people. But actually it is not. Better translation would be:

“To the **holy** and **faithful** breathing…”

Because both words **SAINTS** and **FAITHFUL** describe breathing. It’s not two different groups of people. It is description of the same bothering.

Paul calls Colossians, **holy** and **full of faith** in Christ. He doesn't say you **will become** holy. But **you are** holy. Which will be very important and very relevant for our later studies. Today we will concentrate more on the next bit. Verses 3-8:

### STORY
Since we have a limited time on Sunday, we’re not gonna walk over every observation that can be made. I highly recommend you to do that in your spare time. But, today we will try to concentrate on one aspect.

This verses briefly explain the story how and why this letter have been written.

They introduce new character **Epaphras**. One of the key figures of this story. He was one of the people who brought the gospel to Colossae initially. Probably he worked with Paul for a while in the past. Before he went back home and preached there.  

And people in Colossae responded well to the Gospel. New fellowship of believers were formed. It was a part of a global event when gospel were spread all over the world increasing and bearing the fruit.

It was great. Everything was going very well. Until it was not. Until Fellowship faced some problems. False teachers with *Christian like* teachings. Spiritual leaders with mysterious and sacred knowledge. Philosophy and etc..

New people joined the fellowship and demanded authority and attention. Having their own ideas of the Gospel, of God and how you should follow Him.

### THE PROBLEM
And you know what was the problem? They all sounded appealing. Those people could talk well. Could be persuasive. And it became a real challenge. Especially for relatively new fellowship of believers.

It seems to me that authority of Epaphras were neglected. And that’s when he reached out to Paul for help. To help people in Colossae to find the truth. Help them to figure out what the will of God really is.

### OUR FELLOWSHIP
And it's not that uncommon to this day. In our own fellowship we have this problem arising every so often. People come with they own, bleeding edge ideas about God. They claim to have understanding beyond mere mortals. They have revelations, prophesies and etc... It happened more then once or twice... Early on we had a guy who among other ridiculous claims, claimed to be the Holy Spirit...

And often they actually can speak well. Well, at least they can speak a lot and for the very long time. They can be persuasive and make you doubt...

They make you wonder were is the truth...

### TRUTH
And that's exactly what's happening in our scripture. I want us to pay attention how much the words **truth** and **knowledge** comes up in this 12 verses:

Is the verse 5 the **Gospel** is called **the word of truth**. Then in the verse 6 Paul talks about understanding the grace of God in **TRUTH**. Which means understanding it as it is for real. Understanding things as they are.

Verse 7 - Paul confirms that the **TRUE** gospel is the one they've learned for Epaphras in the beginning.

And then in verse 8. Epaphras informs Paul about the progress fellowship make. How far they've come since they believed. Along with that he speaks about the challenges people experiencing.  

That's why in verse 9 we read what we read:

### Colossians 1:9-12 (NASB95)
9 For this reason also, since the day we heard of it, we have not ceased to pray for you and to ask that you may be filled with the **knowledge** of His will in all **spiritual wisdom** and **understanding**, 10 so that you will walk in a manner worthy of the Lord, to please Him in all respects, bearing fruit in every good work and increasing in the **knowledge** of God...

Again, knowledge and truth all over the place.
