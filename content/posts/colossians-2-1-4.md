## Colossians 2:1-4

### Intro
Greetings, dear brothers and sisters. We continue to explore and dig into the letter to Colossians. Last Sunday we’re finished the chapter 1. So today we’re gonna pick up from there and continue forward.

##### Colossians 2:1-5 (NASB95)
> 1 For I want you to know how great a struggle I have on your behalf and for those who are at Laodicea, and for all those who have not personally seen my face, 2 that their hearts may be encouraged, having been knit together in love, and attaining to all the wealth that comes from the full assurance of understanding, resulting in a true knowledge of God’s mystery, that is, Christ Himself, 3 in whom are hidden all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge.

Great scripture. A lot of big words and long sentences. Let’s start breaking it down... First couple verses are pretty packed. And might be a bit hard to process all at once. Let’s see what they say:

##### Colossians 2:1 (NASB95)
> For I want you to know how great a struggle I have on your behalf and for those who are at Laodicea, and for all those who have not personally seen my face

In this verse, Paul continues to explain  his calling and his ministry. He uses very popular and quite understandable image for those days - sport.

All kids of sport events were extremely popular. Maybe they were the only true entertainment. Gladiators fights, wrestling, running, you name it, it all was there. And bigger the event. Bigger the arena and therefore bigger the importance and the impact. 

### The Arena
In verse 1 Paul quite literally says: “I want you to **see** the magnitude, to realize the size of the arena I’m wrestling in.”

It might not sound to appealing to us, unless you are big sport fan. But back in the day it was extremely relevant to the people.

We see the image of a wrestler, gladiator if you will. Who fights, who wrestles and struggle not for the fame and glory, but for all the believers. For those who know him personally, as well as for those who have never seen him at all.

This image perfectly describes Paul's life and ministry. If you read the book of acts you can learn about all the struggles, hardships and adventures he had to endure. Also, you can find some bits and pieces in his letters to churches. 

It was hard, sometimes brutal, but it was necessary. It was the will of God for him. God gave him special **responsibility** and with that very special **authority**. To be his warrior, his gladiator who fights for the Gospel. And as a result his letters and story still impacts the followers of Christ 2000 years later.

His life was a struggle, but it was for our sake. He suffered so that the Gospel could reach me and you. All the imprisonments, all the losses, all that beating. Was not the result of a sin. It was not a punishment. But it was price that he was willing to pay to see the Gospel preached, to see people saved, to see kingdom of God expand.

That is the Arena and the struggle that he talks about in the verse 1.

And all this made possible through “agape“ - divine love. Selfless love expressed through action. That’s the glue which holds everything together.

That’s the fist step. To introduce people to  the love of God. To help them be united in His love. To bring those seemingly conflicting things together.

##### Colossians 2:4 (NASB95)
> I say this so that no one will delude you with persuasive argument.

Oh... Apparently modern problems are not that modern after all. A couple of weeks ago we started the topic:

**Not everyone who can speak well, is worth listening to…**

And verse 4 goes the same direction. There is two important words that hide behind the English translation.

First is translated as delude, or other translations say mislead. In Greek the word sounds like a **paralogizomai**.

#### Paralogism
Which later became a term that we use to this day - **paralogizm**.  Invalid or false, argument, that appears to be logically correct. 

Effectively it’s an unintentional logical mistake. A piece of faulty or flawed reasoning that is accidental. It occurs when someone makes a logical error in their argument without intending to deceive or trick anyone.

A simple example of such error can be this:

> "Bill Gates and Mark Zuckerberg dropped out of college and became billionaires. Therefore, dropping out of college is a great path to becoming rich."

Sounds logical, yet not correct. But even seemingly innocent errors, can be fatal:

Did you know that George Washington (you can find his portrait on one dollar bill ) was killed by paralogism.

### George Washington
67-year-old George Washington got sick after riding a horse for hours in a bed weather.

Desperate to save the former president, his medical team implemented the absolute pinnacle of late-18th-century medical science. Over a span of roughly 12 hours, they performed Massive Bloodletting: They drained roughly 40% of his entire bodily blood volume.

But instead of helping. It actually became one of the major causes of his death.

The doctors did not mean to harm Washington; At the time, Western medicine believed that you are healthy if 4 liquids of your body are in balance. And if you are sick, it means that they got out of balance. Therefore to help you to heal, balance needs to be restored. 

Their logic was perfectly consistent with their theory, but entirely detached from biological reality.

Everyone, even most educated and the most sharp minds are subjected to paralogisms. History proved it true time and time again. Worst part is that the person himself stays completely blind to his mistake. And genuinely believes that he is right... Basically, we can fool ourselves and not even know about it.

That's the danger #1.

#### Pithanologia/Sophistry
The other word that hides behind "persuasive arguments" is **pithanologia**. Or as we know it today - **sophistry**. Basically it's a clever argument that sounds true and smart, but is actually false and meant to trick people.

So, if paralogism - is unintentional mistake. Sophistry - is intentional. It is well though through way to confuse use. To hide a lie behind arguments that sound perfectly logical...

Lots of industries heavily rely on sophistry. For example in advartisement. They say thing like: "Sugar free..." or "100% All natural". Tricking you into thinking "if it's 100% natural, then it is good". Who have fallen for that one? I want you to consider following *Cyanide* is 100% natural too... Right.

So, Sophistry - intentional wording meant to confuse you.

Unfortunately Christian world is not an exception. People do exactly that. They combine true statements and vague phrases to trick you into believing things bible never says.

The easiest example that I already used toady. Is when people take spiritual metaphor. Like a parable of a sower. And turn it into a business model. They go like this. Bible says:

So, if you struggle financially. You, have to sow more, you have to give more. Invest into our ministry and God will have too bless you because he always stay true to his word...

It sounds logical... But this is not the bible. It's sophistry designed to take an advantage of you. That's why we have to come back to the source. To Jesus:
